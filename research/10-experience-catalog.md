# Sprout — First Experience Catalog

**Version:** 0.1 · **Date:** 2026-11-03 · **Status:** authored proposal for review and playtesting; not an implemented or validated curriculum.

## Product direction

**Little adventures that build real skills.** Children choose purposeful activities; parents can understand what those activities help them practice. Characters are optional. The catalog is shared by girls and boys, with varied interests available to everyone.

The working audience is **ages 3–6**, centered on 4–5 and tested initially with supervised younger children. The [age-range research note](research/03-age-range-and-inclusion.md) explains the recommendation and its limits. Ages 7–11 are an expansion hypothesis, not covered by this deliverable.

This proposal follows the user's 2026-11-03 direction: broaden beyond mathematics and do not require characters. It replaces those assumptions in the older exploration for this design work. Age bands, activity order, duration, navigation, and progression rules below remain proposals. Voice-only, no-reading child gameplay remains the established starting constraint.

## How the catalog relates to curriculum

| Term | Meaning in Sprout |
| --- | --- |
| Curriculum | Intended learning coverage and how understanding can deepen |
| Skill | An observable competency, with a specific context and evidence requirement |
| Experience | An activity a child can choose, potentially exercising several skills |
| Theme | Setting, objects, art, and narrative framing within validated learning bounds |
| Challenge | One authored opportunity within an experience |
| Recommendation | A currently useful choice among eligible experiences |

Experiences and skills have a many-to-many relationship. Concert Crew and Ice Cream Shop can both practice quantity; Ice Cream Shop can also exercise instruction-following. Evidence stays attached to the skill and challenge that produced it. A new theme is not automatically a new learning objective.

## Candidate cards

### EXP-04 — Ice Cream Shop

**Purpose:** prepare pretend orders and see spoken decisions change the cone. **Source:** [issue #95](https://github.com/hurley87/sprout/issues/95).

**Learning:** counting small sets; separately, recognizing an attribute and following an ordered instruction. **Readiness:** understands the object/colour vocabulary and can communicate a distinct choice. **Action:** answer a quantity question, identify a scoop, then name the next ordered step. **Evidence:** separate observations per skill; completion is not combined mastery.

**Authoring repair before playtest:** “Which scoop is pink?” followed by “pink” can be an echo rather than an identifiable selection. Use a discriminating response, such as “the star-marked scoop” versus “the striped scoop,” after checking those labels are understood. Keep markings neutral rather than predictably tied to colour. If that response is too demanding, reframe the activity and its evidence claim. Recognizable strawberry/lemon motifs can improve usability but may become alternate cues to colour.

For the two-step order, record whether the instruction was repeated or the next step was supplied. Prompting each step separately can support ordered participation; it does not establish unaided recall of the whole instruction. **Duration hypothesis:** 3–5 minutes. **Next decision:** author and review these response contracts before reusing a counting rubric.

### EXP-05 — Shape Workshop

**Purpose:** finish a roof, window, or picture. **Learning:** GEO-01. **Readiness:** can select among two named or neutrally labeled pieces. **Action:** choose a shape, then encounter it at a changed size/orientation. **Teaching:** show one example, compare attributes, then remove the cue. **Evidence:** identification in a fresh configuration, with vocabulary support recorded. A roof silhouette that only admits a triangle is useful practice but a cue-assisted task. **Duration:** 3–5 minutes. **Next decision:** author the three-shape item bank and distinguish shape recognition from simply matching an outline.

### EXP-06 — Pattern Parade

**Purpose:** complete a procession of drums and flags. **Learning:** PAT-01. **Readiness:** distinguishes and names the two items. **Action:** say which comes next in an AB sequence. **Teaching:** model the repeating unit, then give a new sequence. **Evidence:** extend more than one independently, with starting item and response position varied. A rhythm or animation cue must not give away the answer. **Duration:** 3–5 minutes. **Next decision:** test static visual patterns before combining sound and movement.

### EXP-07 — Story Detective

**Purpose:** repair a mixed-up picture story. **Learning:** LIT-02; vocabulary incidental unless separately targeted. **Readiness:** understands a brief spoken story and the pictured events. **Action:** say what happened first and next, or choose an event by its content. **Teaching:** model ordering on a separate two-event story. **Evidence:** an ordered reconstruction of a fresh authored story, with prompts recorded. **Duration:** 4–6 minutes. **Next decision:** write two culturally understandable stories and an acceptance rubric that permits paraphrase.

### EXP-08 — Little Nature Lab

**Purpose:** investigate what changes in a simple modeled scene. **Learning:** SCI-01. **Readiness:** understands “before,” “after,” and relevant visible attributes. **Action:** predict, request an authored demonstration, describe the observed difference. **Teaching:** model noticing a change. **Evidence:** an observation supported by what was shown, not whether the initial prediction matched the outcome. **Duration:** 4–6 minutes. **Next decision:** select a scientifically accurate phenomenon and have the depiction reviewed. A plant-growth animation needs an explicit time-lapse explanation; it must not imply instant growth. Optional physical follow-ups require an adult and produce separately attributed observations.

### EXP-09 — Sorting Station

**Purpose:** organize a collection for a pretend museum or workshop. **Learning:** SCI-02. **Readiness:** recognizes the sorting attribute and category vocabulary. **Action:** choose a destination for each visible object by voice. **Teaching:** model the rule and one contrasting case. **Evidence:** classifications of unfamiliar items under the same rule. **Duration:** 3–5 minutes. **Next decision:** vary irrelevant colour/position so they cannot substitute for the intended rule; consider rule-switching only in a later version.

### EXP-10 — Delivery Route

**Purpose:** put a parcel in the right place. **Learning:** GEO-02; LANG-01 only when explicitly tested. **Readiness:** understands the relevant objects and a single spatial term. **Action:** give an authored direction such as “under the bench.” **Teaching:** demonstrate above/below with contrasting scenes. **Evidence:** accurate directions in a changed scene; short phrases accepted. **Duration:** 3–5 minutes. **Next decision:** avoid left/right or multi-step routes until single relations are understood.

## Learning breadth and intentional gaps

The catalog covers early mathematics, language/listening, reasoning, and scientific observation. Creative choices can appear within every activity; Story Detective can later gain a separate story-making mode without scoring creativity as correctness. Music is initially a setting, not evidence of musical competence.

Social relationships, physical development, handwriting, comprehensive reading instruction, and general executive-function improvement are not claimed outcomes. Those require other activities and evidence. This is a supplemental experience catalog.

## Authoring and build order

1. Review the three worked cards with an early-childhood educator and caregivers. Resolve unclear vocabulary and response contracts.
2. Instrument the existing counting baseline using [issue #19](https://github.com/hurley87/sprout/issues/19) before running and interpreting new-domain experiments.
3. Test quantity comparison, then rhyme recognition, following [issue #16](https://github.com/hurley87/sprout/issues/16).
4. Develop the mixed-skill Ice Cream Shop and Shape Workshop next, using findings from those experiments.
5. Expand into the other candidates only after the interaction and evidence are credible.

This prioritizes shape authoring sooner than #16's broader roadmap, in response to the user's stated interest. It does not change #16's first two new-domain experiments. Its remaining reference roadmap is patterns/reasoning → spatial/geometry → story comprehension → science/curiosity → executive-function games. Those are experiment directions, not a compulsory sequence for children.

The three worked designs intentionally do not define a universal lesson engine, complex recommender, or exhaustive skill graph. See the [shared/domain boundary](11-skill-map-and-progression.md#minimum-shared-and-domain-boundary), [authoring template](experiences/TEMPLATE.md), and [pilot plan](12-pilot-and-validation.md).
