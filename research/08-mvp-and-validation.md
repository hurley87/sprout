# Sprout — MVP and Validation

> **2026-11-03 update:** This is the earlier math/character MVP. The new [catalog](10-experience-catalog.md) proposes broader learning experiences and optional characters. Its [pilot plan](12-pilot-and-validation.md) uses supervised trials to investigate age suitability, help requirements, and learning evidence; independent use and session duration remain unvalidated hypotheses.

Initial scope, exclusions, and experiments for engagement, learning, repeat use, and payment.

**Status:** Early product exploration and prototyping.

Based on sections 12, 13 of the [original product exploration](source/product-exploration.md), updated with the user’s 2026-10-30 product direction.

## MVP

### Objective

Determine whether young children enjoy and repeatedly engage with an animated voice-driven learning experience, and whether parents perceive enough educational value to pay.

### Initial scope

**One character:** Pip the Fox.

**One subject:** Math, beginning with early number sense.

**One audience:** Preschoolers approximately 3–5 years old.

**One initial adventure:** Counting and comparing quantities in an interactive garden, presented as a progression of game levels.

**Session length:** A working target of 5–10 minutes per lesson, to be validated.

**Access model:** An adult sets up the lesson, then can step away while the child plays independently.

**Child interaction:** Voice-only gameplay with no reading required.

**Visual direction:** Hand-drawn illustrations inspired by [Margin Call](https://margincall.fun/); see [Visuals and Animation](04-visuals-and-animation.md).

### MVP features

- Subject-first home screen with Math as the initial subject.
- Animated instructor with expressive states synchronized to speech.
- Simple illustrated learning environment.
- Structured, authored lessons with level progression.
- Voice-only answers, choices, and progression; no reading or touch interaction required from the child.
- Appropriate hints and correction.
- Reliable start, stop, and session recovery.
- Adult lesson setup, followed by independent child play.
- Basic session recap.
- Minimal measurement of interaction failures and learning evidence.

### Explicitly out of scope

- Full animated character roster.
- Open-ended AI-generated curriculum.
- Large content library.
- Multiplayer experiences.
- Complex reward systems and virtual economies; simple level progression is part of the MVP.
- Banking and financial rewards.
- Advanced parent dashboards.
- Marketplace or instructor creator tools.

## Initial Experiments

### Experiment A — Character engagement

Test with a small number of children, beginning with direct observation of the prototype.

Questions:
- Does the child understand the spoken instructions without reading?
- Can the child progress between levels using only their voice?
- Does the character feel engaging?
- Does the child speak naturally to it?
- Does the character handle interruptions and mistakes?
- Does the child want another activity?
- Can the adult step away after setup, and what causes them to intervene?
- Does the child remain interested through a 5–10 minute lesson, and where does attention fall away?
- Do speech and animation feel synchronized, including during interruptions?

### Experiment B — Repeat usage

Invite a small group of families to use multiple activities over several weeks.

Measure:
- First-session completion.
- Repeated usage.
- Voluntary returns.
- Parent assistance required after initial setup.
- Lesson duration and where children stop or abandon a session.
- Voice interpretation, recovery, and animation synchronization failures.
- Child engagement.
- Parent feedback.

### Experiment C — Learning value

Test whether children can apply practiced concepts to new examples without assistance.

Separate:
- Activity completion.
- Correctness with help.
- Independent demonstration.
- Retention and transfer to new tasks.

### Experiment D — Willingness to pay

Once the experience is reliable enough:
- Test a parent-facing landing page.
- Experiment with a clear value proposition and stated price.
- Run a limited advertising campaign.
- Measure activation, repeat use, purchase conversion, and retention.
- Track acquisition and model-delivery costs.
