# Research note: Head Start Early Learning Outcomes Framework

**Reviewed:** 2026-11-03. **Source type:** official US early-learning framework and goal descriptions. **Scope:** birth to five, with preschool goals most relevant here. This is a targeted source review, not a systematic review of learning-app effectiveness.

## What the framework establishes

The Early Learning Outcomes Framework (ELOF) organizes learning into domains, subdomains, goals, developmental progressions, and observable indicators. Its five central domains are approaches to learning; social and emotional development; language and literacy; cognition; and perceptual, motor, and physical development. Preschool descriptions distinguish language/communication from literacy, and mathematics from scientific reasoning.

Head Start explicitly says the framework must not be used as a curriculum, assessment, or checklist for declaring that a child has failed or is unready for school. Its age indicators allow individual variation. [Source: ELOF introduction and organization](https://headstart.gov/interactive-head-start-early-learning-outcomes-framework-ages-birth-five).

**Sprout interpretation:** use ELOF to check the breadth and precision of proposed learning objectives. Author the actual experiences separately. A mapping below means “relevant to this goal,” not complete coverage, certification, or validated assessment.

ELOF's preschool descriptions commonly distinguish 36–48 and 48–60 months. For rhyme, supported recognition appears before more independent selection and production. Shape descriptions include changes in size and orientation, not just naming familiar pictures. Language guidance recognizes that children may show more complex communication in their home language. These distinctions inform the proposed cards; they do not impose age deadlines. ## Proposed translation into Sprout experiences

These are original product proposals informed by the goals above.

| Curriculum area | Candidate experiences | Evidence Sprout might observe | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Number sense | Concert Crew; Pack a Picnic | A quantity named, or a group selected, with support recorded | A correct total alone does not reveal how the child counted |
| Shapes and space | Shape Workshop; Delivery Route | Selection across changed orientation; a stated spatial relation | Voice choices do not demonstrate physical construction skill |
| Language and listening | Rhyme Radio; Story Detective | A sound-based choice; a story detail or ordered retelling | Familiarity with the words can affect performance |
| Science and discovery | Little Nature Lab; Sorting Station | A prediction, observation, or classification of visible properties | An animation is a model, not an observation of a real physical experiment |
| Approaches to learning | Supported throughout | Asking for help, trying another approach, remembering an instruction | Do not infer a stable attention or executive-function score |
| Social and physical development | Later family activities | Optional caregiver observations, kept separate | A solo voice app cannot claim comprehensive coverage |

Approaches to learning includes self-regulation, attention, memory, flexibility, curiosity, and creativity. Sprout can offer relevant opportunities, but a short session is a narrow context. [Source: Approaches to Learning Preschool](https://headstart.gov/school-readiness/article/approaches-learning-preschool).

## Decisions this research supports

- Define specific skills beneath broad subject labels.
- Distinguish recognizing, naming, applying, and remembering; one response format does not establish all of them.
- Include language and scientific observation in the catalog alongside mathematics.
- Describe support and context in parent recaps.

## Questions this source cannot settle

ELOF does not establish Sprout's optimal age range, lesson duration, voice-recognition reliability, independent-use readiness, commercial demand, or learning effectiveness. Its preschool coverage does not validate activities for ages 6–11. Those are separate design and validation questions.
