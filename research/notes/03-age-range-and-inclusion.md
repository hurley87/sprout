# Research synthesis: Audience, age range, and inclusion

**Date:** 2026-11-03. **Status:** product recommendation for validation, not a developmental assessment or confirmed market decision.

## Recommended scope

Design the first experience catalog for **approximately ages 3–6**, with **ages 4–5 as the design center**. Include three-year-olds in initial supervised testing because the existing concert and shop experiments were written for roughly ages 3–4. Recruit some six-year-olds to investigate where the proposed content becomes too easy or too young in tone.

Treat ages 7–8 as the next possible expansion, and 9–11 as a separate design investigation. The initial research does not justify claiming that one experience format serves ages 3–11 equally well.

## Research basis and limits

Head Start's preschool descriptions are directly relevant to early counting, shapes, sound awareness, and simple instruction-following. NAEYC's guidance is broader and emphasizes individual and contextual variation. Neither source establishes the right commercial audience for a voice-based product. [Head Start research note](01-head-start-framework.md), [NAEYC/IES research note](02-learning-design-evidence.md).

The proposed upper age of six is an adjacent product-testing boundary, not a Head Start benchmark. Older children's curriculum, interests, and interaction needs require additional research before expansion.

## Working design bands

These are planning hypotheses. They must not become rigid skill gates or labels shown to children.

| Band | Proposed experience emphasis | Product questions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 3–4 | Concrete objects; a small set of choices; one actionable prompt; short play with an adult nearby | Is the task understood? Can the speech system handle responses? What help is needed? |
| 5–6 | Connected pretend tasks; short sequences; patterns; sound games; simple explanations | Is there enough challenge and autonomy? Which skills vary most within the band? |
| 7–8 | Investigations; richer stories; construction problems; emerging reading tasks | Does voice remain the best interaction for each task? Does the presentation feel appropriate? |
| 9–11 | Projects; strategy; experiments; more control over goals and tools | What distinctly older-child product would be valuable? |

Start with a **3–5 minute design target** for the first worked experiences, with earlier endings and optional extension. This revises the older notes' 5–10 minute hypothesis for this pilot only. Neither range is a validated attention-span limit. Measure actual duration and useful participation rather than requiring children to stay for a target time.

## Readiness beyond age

Select an entry point using a low-pressure sample and caregiver context, then adapt within authored bounds. Track separately:

- Familiarity with the target concept.
- Understanding of the activity's vocabulary and spoken instructions.
- Ability to communicate a choice through the supported input.
- Familiarity with pictured objects or story settings.
- Amount and type of help needed in this session.

A misunderstanding of “fewer,” an unknown picture name, and a speech-recognition failure are different from evidence about quantity comparison or rhyme recognition. A session that cannot distinguish them should remain inconclusive.

## Inclusion choices for the first catalog

- **One catalog for girls and boys.** Offer music, food, animals, transport, nature, building, and imaginative settings to everyone. Recommendations use demonstrated interests and readiness, not gender assumptions.
- **Optional characters.** A warm voice and responsive scene can carry an experience. No mascot is required for the first experiments.
- **Language-specific first pilot.** The worked rhyme examples are English. Their suitability depends on vocabulary and pronunciation. Do not translate word lists without revalidating the sound relationships and response contract.
- **Voice-only remains the current gameplay constraint.** Record when it prevents participation. Supported participation is valuable, but adult-translated answers must not be reported as independent app performance. Future alternative input needs deliberate design; it is not already supported.
- **Perceptual access.** Use distinguishable silhouettes, clear spacing, replayable audio, and reduced motion. Identify when an activity itself depends on colour or sound discrimination instead of claiming all variants are equally accessible.
- **Parent involvement is an observation.** Supervise early trials. The older goal of independent play after setup remains a hypothesis; it is not established for every three-year-old or every activity.

## What would change the recommendation?

Narrow the first audience if task comprehension or voice reliability fails consistently in a band despite revisions. Expand only when fresh-example performance, repeat participation, and manageable support are observed beyond the initial group. Separate interface failure from unsuitable content before excluding an age band.

An educator review and small family pilot can improve the designs. They cannot establish population-wide developmental norms or causal learning effects.
