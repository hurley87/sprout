# The Invisible Unicorns: Counting Game

Nov 14, 2026 · @David Hurley

## Overview

A unicorn has sneezed glitter onto a magic wand and accidentally made her friends invisible. They are still in the meadow, and each one makes a sound. She listens, counts, and tells Sprout how many friends are hiding.

Counting reveals the picture. When she names the right amount, the unicorns pop into view wearing ridiculous accessories. If she is unsure or names a different amount, a slower replay reveals one unicorn per sound so she can check her count.

The rule stays the same: each hidden unicorn makes exactly one sound in a sequence. A loud sneeze still counts as one. A long trumpet note still counts as one. The game explores quantity through listening, with visible support whenever she needs it.

- **Proposed route:** `/invisible-unicorns`
- **Proposed lesson id:** `counting-invisible-unicorns`
- **Length:** five listening rounds and an interactive finale
- **Counts:** 2 to 4 unicorns
- **Interaction:** voice-only answers and replay requests, with game-controlled sound sequences
- **Learning:** counting separate events, connecting a heard quantity to a visible group, and recognizing that loudness and duration do not change the count

## Listening rounds

The meadow begins empty of visible unicorns in each round. The first sound sequence is her chance to predict the hidden group before seeing it.

| Order | Hidden unicorns | Each one makes | Reveal |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 2 | One friendly neigh | Enormous sunglasses and a pineapple hat |
| 2 | 3 | One hiccup | Three unicorns with tiny moustaches |
| 3 | 2 | One sneeze, one tiny and one theatrical | Handkerchiefs and crooked party hats |
| 4 | 4 | One carrot crunch | Four unicorns wearing chef hats |
| 5 | 3 | One silly trumpet note | A miniature unicorn marching band |
| 6 | She chooses 2 to 4 | One friendly neigh each | Her chosen group hides, calls out, then reappears for a bow |

- **Fixed first version:** use this order and these counts so the listening challenge is predictable to test. Random counts can come later.
- **One sound per friend:** no double hiccups, repeated sneezes, multiple crunches, or strings of trumpet notes. Each sound must be easy to hear as one event.
- **Changing sound, same rule:** the comedy changes without asking her to learn a new way to answer.
- **No rising-volume surprise:** a theatrical sneeze can have a different character without being startlingly loud.
- **Finale is different:** she chooses an amount and watches it happen. There is no hidden-answer test in the finale.

## How a round goes

Sprout explains once: "Oops! Our friends are invisible. Each unicorn will make one sound. Listen, then tell me how many are hiding!"

Round 1 uses two short, clearly separated neighs. Sprout stays quiet during the sequence, then asks, "How many unicorns did you hear?"

| She does | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| Gives the correct number after the sequence | The hidden group appears and Sprout celebrates finding them |
| Gives a different number | Sprout says "Let's find out" and starts a slower, supported replay |
| Says "again" or "listen again" | The same hidden sequence plays again without revealing the group |
| Says "help" or "I don't know" | The game offers or begins the supported replay |
| Counts aloud during the sequence | The game lets the sound sequence finish; her counting does not interrupt it or submit an early answer |
| Gives an unclear answer after the sequence | Sprout asks briefly for a number or offers to play the sounds again |
| Goes quiet after the prompt | Sprout eventually offers "Want to hear them again?" without revealing the answer |

The answer turn opens only after the final sound has finished. Sprout can then hear a short answer such as "three" or a count-up that ends at three. A replay request is always available during that answer turn.

There is no timer, score penalty, or limit on listening again. A replay has the same number and order of sounds; it is another listen to the same group, not a new problem.

## The supported replay

If she names a different quantity or asks for help, make the connection between the sounds and the friends visible.

1. Return to the start of the same sound sequence.
2. Play it slowly, revealing one unicorn as each sound starts.
3. Leave every revealed unicorn on screen in a clear row.
4. Ask, "Now we can see them! How many friends are there?"

If she is still unsure, Sprout counts the visible friends with her, then invites her to say the total. The group stays visible while she answers. Do not hide the unicorns again as a consequence of an incorrect answer.

- **No false reveal:** always reveal the actual group. Her guess never changes how many unicorns were hiding.
- **No fake agreement:** Sprout does not celebrate an incorrect total as correct; it responds with a chance to check.
- **Same success celebration:** an independent answer and an answer with help both lead to the happy reveal moment and the next round.
- **No answer required to request help:** "again" and "help" are useful actions in their own right.

## Optional hoofprint support

If counting sounds from memory is too difficult, each sound can leave one glowing hoofprint marker in the meadow. The markers stay visible, so she can count them after the sequence.

- Use one marker per sound, not a pair or trail of hoofprints.
- Start a replay with an empty marker row and rebuild it; do not accumulate markers across listens.
- Keep the markers separate from background decoration and use a consistent left-to-right order.
- In this mode, the task becomes counting heard events with visual support. Treat that as a useful version of the game, not a lesser result.
- Offer the support through a simple adult setting or a clear help choice. Do not silently change modes after a fixed number of mistakes.

Hoofprints support the initial listening sequence. The supported replay goes further by revealing the actual unicorns. Once unicorns are visible, remove or visually retire the markers so there are not two competing sets to count.

## The interactive finale

The wand is working again, and she gets to choose the last magic trick.

Sprout asks, "How many friends should we hide: two, three, or four?"

- Her chosen group appears first, so she can see what she requested.
- The unicorns turn invisible.
- Each one gives one friendly neigh.
- The group reappears for a bow under a rainbow.

If she requests a number outside 2 to 4, Sprout explains the available choice and asks again. Do not silently substitute another amount. If she asks Sprout to choose, it can pick three and say so.

The finale celebrates her controlling the magic. There is no follow-up quiz and no need to get another answer right before finishing.

## What she sees and hears

Use one pink and lavender meadow with a small castle far away, a glittery wand near the edge, and an open middle for the hidden group.

- **Hidden state:** the middle looks empty. Do not show silhouettes, individual shadows, hats, or numbered hiding places that disclose the quantity before the sounds.
- **Unicorns:** white with pink and purple manes, large enough to count on a phone. Accessories add personality without looking like extra countable characters.
- **Sound playback:** one clear event at a time, with a short quiet gap between events and no overlap. Use roughly a second of quiet as a starting point, then tune with her.
- **Sound design:** a neigh, hiccup, sneeze, crunch, or trumpet note must register as one unit. Avoid echoes, repeated attacks, or laughter that sounds like extra events.
- **Volume and duration:** introduce differences gently. Never make a sound so quiet that hearing it becomes the challenge, or so elaborate that it sounds like several unicorns.
- **Background audio:** no music, animal calls, or decorative sound effects during the listening sequence. The listening task needs an uncluttered sound field.
- **Correct reveal:** the group appears together after the answer. The supported replay instead reveals one friend per sound.
- **Celebration:** accessory wiggles, a small sparkle burst, and a short tutor response happen after the listening task has ended. Avoid extra neighs that could be mistaken for more clues.
- **Glow cue:** use the shared glowing unicorn horn, with a distinct listening-to-the-game state during playback and the normal child-turn state afterward. Retain accessible status text.
- **Reduced motion:** replace pop-ins and wiggles with static appearances. Preserve sound timing and leave the final group visible.

Provide a visual-support mode for children who cannot use the audio-only version. Its learning task is supported counting, rather than an auditory-memory test. In audio-only play, accessible labels must not accidentally announce the hidden total before the answer; communicate the phase and replay/help options without giving it away.

## Runtime notes

These are proposed behaviors, not verified claims about the current code. Check lesson definitions, audio playback ownership, voice-turn handling, and render acknowledgment before choosing an implementation approach.

**Round facts and phases**

Each listening round has a fixed hidden count, an ordered list of sound events, a reveal group with exactly that many unicorns, and accessory data. Each sound event belongs to exactly one unicorn.

The logical phases are introduction, hidden playback, answer, optional supported playback, supported answer, and celebration. The finale has its own choose, hide, playback, and bow sequence.

- Validate that the hidden count, sound-event count, and reveal-object count agree.
- Keep the round's count fixed through replays, help, and incorrect answers.
- Advance only after a correct answer in the independent or supported answer phase; the finale completes after its chosen sequence.
- Represent hoofprint support explicitly so its behavior is predictable and testable.

**Own the countable audio**

Use authored, reviewed sound clips played by the app for the countable events. Do not ask the live tutor to improvise the exact number of sneezes or hiccups: an extra vocalization would change the problem.

- Sprout finishes its introduction before playback begins and stays silent throughout the sequence.
- Schedule the next event after the previous clip completes plus the intended gap, so different clip durations cannot cause overlap.
- Open the answer turn only after the final clip completes.
- Prevent game audio from being mistaken for child speech or triggering an answer.
- Define how speech during playback is handled without interrupting the sequence or leaking an early partial answer into the next phase. Do not assume the current voice runtime already supports this.
- If playback fails or is interrupted, do not ask her to answer an incomplete sequence. Explain briefly and offer a complete replay of the same round.
- Cancel pending playback on navigation or round changes, and ignore stale completion callbacks.

**Interpret the answer**

Recognize a final quantity, replay request, help request, or unclear response. The actual expected number comes from round state, not from the tutor's judgment. Check a resolved numerical answer against that number deterministically.

Answer interpretation must preserve the difference between replaying invisibly and revealing with help. Repeated transcripts must not start overlapping replays or advance multiple rounds.

**Render and audio coordination**

For supported playback, reveal one unicorn with each corresponding sound and retain it for the rest of the sequence. On replay, reset the reveal state before the first event. Test this on a real device: a visibly delayed unicorn could weaken the one-sound-one-friend relationship.

**A small first experiment**

Before making all the artwork and audio, try one round with three friendly neighs and three simple unicorn illustrations. Check whether she understands that each sound belongs to a different hidden friend, can request a replay, and enjoys discovering the group. Then test a count of two so she cannot succeed by remembering a single answer.

## What to test with her

The biggest question is whether the invisible-friends story makes the sounds feel countable, rather than like one unicorn making noise repeatedly.

- [ ] **One sound, one friend:** she understands that each sound in a sequence comes from a different hidden unicorn.
- [ ] **The reveal is worth it:** she wants to discover the silly costumes and asks to play another round.
- [ ] **Small quantities work:** 2 to 4 sounds are manageable without turning the game into a frustrating memory test.
- [ ] **The gaps feel right:** sounds are clearly separate without making her lose track while waiting.
- [ ] **Each clip counts as one:** sneezes, crunches, and trumpet notes do not contain misleading extra beats.
- [ ] **Different sounds, same quantity:** a longer or more theatrical sound does not make her count an extra unicorn.
- [ ] **Replay is understood:** "again" gives another listen to the same group, and she does not add both listens together.
- [ ] **Thinking aloud is safe:** counting during playback neither interrupts the audio nor submits a premature answer.
- [ ] **Supported replay helps:** revealing one friend per sound makes the connection clearer.
- [ ] **Hoofprints help when needed:** one marker per sound supports counting without introducing a second confusing set.
- [ ] **Short answers are heard:** quick number words and "again" register reliably after playback.
- [ ] **Phone audio works:** the quietest clip is audible in an ordinary room, with no startling volume jumps.
- [ ] **Finale choice is satisfying:** she understands that her number determines how many friends hide.
- [ ] **Length:** five rounds and a finale stay fun. If attention drops, try rounds 1, 3, and 4 followed by the finale.
