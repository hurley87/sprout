Learning principles, a math-first curriculum, structured lessons and levels, and learning evidence.

**Status:** Early product exploration and prototyping.

Based on sections 6, 7 of the [original product exploration](source/product-exploration.md), updated with the user’s 2026-10-30 product direction.

## Learning Philosophy

Sprout should combine engaging characters with sound educational principles.

### Key principles

**Active participation**
Children should speak, count, explain, choose, and interact.

**Learning through play**
Activities should feel like adventures rather than formal exercises.

**Guided discovery**
Instructors should help children find answers rather than immediately supplying them.

**Mistakes are opportunities**
Wrong answers trigger helpful feedback and further exploration.

**Age-appropriate pacing**
Short questions, simple language, thinking time, and natural pauses. Aim for 5–10 minute lessons as a working hypothesis, with frequent opportunities to participate and a natural ending. Validate the duration through observation.

**Observable understanding**
Finishing an activity is not automatically proof of learning.

**Healthy engagement**
Design for useful, enjoyable sessions with natural stopping points—not endless screen time or emotional dependence on characters.

### Learning evidence

Sprout should distinguish:
- Correct independent responses.
- Correct responses following assistance.
- Partial understanding.
- Misunderstandings.
- Unclear or unverified responses.
- Activities skipped or abandoned.

Parents should receive understandable summaries without overstating what the child has learned.

## Content and Curriculum

### Initial subject and future categories

**Math is the only initial subject.** Start with early number sense: counting and comparing quantities. The wider math topics below are potential extensions, not a commitment to include all of them in the first release. Language, science, and creativity remain future possibilities.

**Early mathematics**
- Counting
- Number recognition
- Comparing quantities
- Shapes
- Patterns
- Simple addition and subtraction

**Language and literacy**
- Vocabulary
- Story comprehension
- Rhyming
- Phonological awareness
- Sequencing events

**Science and discovery**
- Animals
- Nature
- Weather
- Observation
- Prediction and explanation

**Creativity and reasoning**
- Storytelling
- Problem-solving
- Imagination
- Spatial reasoning
- Simple puzzles

### Lesson structure

Each lesson must have an authored structure. Its content definition should include:

- Learning objectives.
- Required concepts or skills.
- Visual scenes and the sequence of challenges or levels.
- Spoken instructions that require no reading.
- Expected learner responses.
- Acceptable variations.
- Common misunderstandings.
- Available hints and guidance.
- Level transition and lesson completion conditions.
- Repeat prompts and recovery for unclear speech.
- A planned duration and natural stopping point.
- Evidence captured for the parent recap.

Lessons can share a technical foundation while retaining carefully authored educational content.

### Proposed lesson flow

A useful starting structure is: **spoken introduction → short challenges → guidance and retries → independent check → recap and finish**. This is a proposed authoring pattern, not a finalized curriculum.

The working content hierarchy is **Subject → lesson → levels/challenges**. A lesson is tentatively one 5–10 minute session containing a short progression. Whether a level corresponds to a whole lesson or a smaller step remains open; the duration target must not silently become a requirement for every individual challenge.

The child completes the progression entirely by voice. Game progression and demonstrated understanding should remain separate: completing a level after a hint is different evidence from answering independently.
