# Experience authoring template

**Status:** proposal / reviewed / implemented / piloted. **Version/date:** record explicitly. **Findings:** distinguish observed results from expectations.

## Purpose and learning contract

- Experience ID, child-facing name, and child goal.
- Primary skill and separately observed secondary skills.
- Suggested age/developmental entry; language, sensory, and input requirements.
- Research connection and limits of the alignment.
- Product hypothesis and what the experiment should teach us.
- Duration hypothesis and natural stopping points.
- Pedagogical stage: introduction, supported recognition, independent response, application, review, etc.
- Representation: objects, pictures, spoken sounds, symbols, or another appropriate form.
- What success can establish, and what it cannot.

## Authored sequence

| Step/item ID | Scene or audio | Exact task/example prompt | Permitted response and target | Evidence/support label | Next action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Populate before pilot | Include counts, labels, positions, and pronunciation where relevant | One actionable request | Accept paraphrases only within an explicit contract | Demonstration, supported, fresh, or delayed | Correct/incorrect/unclear/help/stop behavior |

Include a separate demonstration example, fresh equivalents, and a later review variant. Define how response positions and irrelevant cues vary. Use real examples, not just a description of a generator.

## Teaching and control of error

- What the child does that meaningfully changes the activity.
- Whether the scene can reveal a discrepancy; whether that cue is present before or after an initial response.
- Wait/repeat/re-present/hint/simplify/model options.
- A bounded retry policy and an acceptable way to move on without success.
- Recognition versus naming/recall distinctions.

## Evidence and progression

- Observable response, scene identity, support actually delivered, and attribution.
- Independent versus supported success; uncertainty and parent assistance.
- What can adapt during the activity and what must remain fixed.
- Eligibility for optional challenge versus mastery claims.
- Review selection and stop behavior; use the [shared policy](../11-skill-map-and-progression.md).
- Example truthful parent recap.

## Presentation and generation

- Required visual/audio assets and accessible distinctions.
- What may vary: theme, framing, wording, or approved examples.
- What must be validated: learning target, answer, cues, prerequisites, and equivalence.
- Failed-validation fallback: authored known-good item, not unchecked improvisation.

## Validation and findings

- Key failure modes and confounds.
- What would justify continuing, revising, or stopping investment.
- Instrumentation and any live-provider/child checks not yet performed.
- Actual findings with dates and limitations; write “not yet tested” until evidence exists.
