# EXP-01 — Concert Crew

**Version:** concert-crew-teaching-v1 · **Date:** 2026-11-03 · **Status:** fully authored design; not implemented or playtested.

## Purpose and scope

**Child goal:** get a small concert stage ready. Each completed task places the equipment in its intended spot; the child chooses an optional final stage decoration by voice. No mascot or celebrity likeness is needed.

**Primary skill:** NUM-01, naming the quantity of a visible set of 1–3 objects. **Optional extension:** quantities 4–6. **Suggested entry:** ages 3–5, with an introductory version available whenever appropriate. **Readiness:** understands “how many,” recognizes the pictured objects, and can communicate number words. Reading, numerals, and arithmetic are unnecessary.

**Research connection:** a narrow opportunity related to counting/cardinality in Head Start P-MATH 3; see the [research note](../research/01-head-start-framework.md). Naming a total does not establish the child's counting strategy or mastery of the whole goal.

**Hypothesis:** a visible preparation goal and useful support will sustain participation and produce interpretable fresh quantity responses. **Duration hypothesis:** 3–5 minutes, with optional extension and an earlier ending always available.

### Relationship to issue #94

[Issue #94](https://github.com/hurley87/sprout/issues/94) specifies a seven-round counting theme with quantities **1, 2, 3, 3, 4, 5, 6**. Preserve that as a separately identified baseline. This document proposes a teaching variant with a demonstration, a bounded core, and optional harder rounds. It does not silently change #94 or claim its implementation exists.

## Pedagogy and representation

Use individually visible illustrated objects. The core moves from brief modeling, through supported practice as needed, to fresh quantity responses. Later retrieval changes the objects and layout. Numeral recognition and symbolic arithmetic remain separate future objectives.

The child's protected cognitive work is determining the total. Before the first response, the tutor must not say the total, enumerate the objects, display a numeral, or show an equal-sized set of numbered slots. A spoken invitation to “count” does not itself demonstrate that the child used one-to-one counting.

## Authored scene and task sequence

Objects remain still during the response opportunity. Stage decoration must not contain copies of target objects. Each row has a unique scene ID.

| ID / role | Scene | Example spoken task | Target | Evidence and action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| C0 / welcome | Empty stage; no countable task objects | “Let's get the stage ready. You can ask for help or say stop.” | No evaluated answer | Invitation only |
| C1 / entry opportunity | One microphone, centered | “How many microphones are here?” | 1 | Initial independent opportunity if understood; place microphone after accepted success, or teach if needed |
| CD / conditional demonstration | Two speakers, separate and identical | “Watch: one, two. There are two speakers altogether.” | No evaluated answer | Highlight each speaker in sync; model the meaning of the final total; never score an echo |
| C2 / practice | Two guitars in a row | “How many guitars are here?” | 2 | Fresh objects after CD if used; record any support. Move them into stands after accepted success |
| C3 / fresh opportunity | Three bracelets in a horizontal row | “How many bracelets are here?” | 3 | Record first response and support. Place bracelets on a display after success or explicitly modeled completion |
| C4 / changed arrangement | Three stars in a spaced triangular arrangement | “How many stars are here?” | 3 | Fresh arrangement; not proof of full quantity invariance. Hang the stars after success or modeled completion |
| CE4 / optional challenge | Four stage lights in a 2-by-2 arrangement | “How many lights are here?” | 4 | Extension only when requested and appropriate; classify separately from the 1–3 core |
| CE5 / optional challenge | Five large beads, separated in one row | “How many beads are here?” | 5 | No decorative beads elsewhere; same evidence rules |
| CE6 / optional challenge | Six butterflies, spaced in two rows of three, motion paused | “How many butterflies are here?” | 6 | No overlap; same evidence rules |
| CF / ending | Completed stage | “We got the stage ready. Would you like stars or ribbons for the finish?” | Either choice | Preference only; gentle celebration, then clear ending |

**Branching:** if C1 shows unfamiliarity or the child asks for help, use CD before C2. Otherwise begin C2 without a demonstration and introduce CD only when needed. After C4, offer the ending. Offer CE4 only if the child wants another challenge and the core has supplied at least two fresh independent responses; advance through CE5/CE6 only with continued willingness and an independent response at the preceding extension. Stop an extension after bounded support and finish warmly. These are pilot rules, not developmental thresholds.

## Response contract

- Accept the target number alone or a clear equivalent phrase, such as “three stars.”
- Counting aloud can be participation; if the final total is not clear, ask “How many altogether?” without supplying it. Record that follow-up.
- “Yes,” a repeated object name, silence, or unclear audio does not establish quantity.
- Preserve a self-correction made before a substantive hint. Do not accept a tutor-supplied answer as the child's independent contribution.
- Caregiver answers or coaching are marked separately. If speaker attribution is uncertain, the observation stays unverified.

## Support and control of error

After thinking time, offer one neutral replay of the prompt. If the response remains unclear, clarify once and then offer help or ending; do not convert the uncertainty into a wrong answer.

For an interpretable mismatch or a request for help:

1. Invite another look without stating the total: “Let's make sure each one gets counted once.”
2. If needed, highlight objects one at a time while the child counts. This is substantive support; object tracking may reduce the task demand.
3. If still needed, demonstrate the full count and total. Mark the result as modeled, then move on without forcing repetition.

Allow at most two child response opportunities on an item after its initial attempt. Stop sooner on request or clear frustration. The highlights help avoid double-counting, but the initial spoken total has no complete environmental control of error. Do not pretend otherwise.

Use at most one immediate fresh probe after supported success; do not extend the session just to collect evidence. A quantity-2 response does not resolve uncertainty at quantity 3. Later review is optional and follows the [shared selection policy](../11-skill-map-and-progression.md).

## Evidence, completion, and recap

Record quantity, arrangement, object type, first response, delivered support, self-correction, final response, and completion mode. Finishing can follow independent success, help, demonstration, or an early ending; those remain distinct.

**Example recap:** “Practiced naming groups of 1–3. Named two guitars independently; needed help with the bracelets; named three stars in a new arrangement without help.” Only use this wording if those events occurred.

## Variation and validation

Original garden, concert, or workshop art can provide theme variants. Hold quantity, spacing, visibility, object familiarity, and support policy comparable when testing a theme. Complex bracelets, clusters, background duplicates, or moving targets can change difficulty.

For this pilot, instructional scenes and answer keys are fixed. AI may paraphrase short invitations and acknowledgments within the contract. It must not generate quantities, hints that disclose the first answer, or progression rules. If an item/scene cannot be validated, use its known-good authored version or end that item.

## Success criteria and findings

Continue investing if children understand the preparation activity, the system records their intended answers accurately, and fresh responses are interpretable with manageable help. Revise if decorative objects confuse counts, the tutor gives away totals, or speech failures dominate. Enjoyment and correct answers alone do not establish learning gains.

Use the [pilot plan](../12-pilot-and-validation.md). **Findings: not yet tested.** Live microphone behavior, rendered assets, response timing, and child outcomes have not been verified by this design document.
