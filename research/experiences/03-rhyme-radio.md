# EXP-03 — Rhyme Radio

**Version:** rhyme-radio-english-v1 · **Date:** 2026-11-03 · **Status:** fully authored design; not implemented or playtested. Word/audio review is required before child sessions.

## Purpose and learning contract

**Child goal:** choose rhyming sound pairs for a playful radio show. An accepted choice adds two pictures to the broadcast board, followed by a brief spoken rhyme. The child chooses a final sound effect from two approved options. No character or reading is required.

**Primary skill:** LIT-01, recognizing a rhyming option from two spoken candidates. **Suggested entry:** ages 4–6, with supported exploration available for younger children. **Readiness:** understands the relevant spoken words, can hear the presented contrast, and can communicate a distinct option. Counting and letter knowledge are not prerequisites.

**Research connection:** a narrow sound-awareness opportunity related to P-LIT 1. Recognition, rhyme production, initial-sound identification, decoding, and reading comprehension are different targets. [Head Start research note](../research/01-head-start-framework.md).

**Hypothesis:** short, purposeful sound choices can support participation and produce fresh rhyme-recognition evidence beyond the counting domain. **Duration hypothesis:** 3–5 minutes. **Representation:** spoken words plus simple supporting pictures; pictures identify options but do not determine the sound relationship.

## Pronunciation and vocabulary preparation

Use a reviewed English pronunciation for each word; verify both alternatives are familiar enough to interpret. Picture labels are spoken neutrally, in the same voice, loudness, and pace. Do not exaggerate only the correct word, sing the answer, or use a rhyming carrier sentence that supplies it.

If the child uses a legitimate alternate picture label, such as “doggy” for “dog,” establish the intended spoken label for the item. Record this help. Do not mark a response wrong because a different label changes the rhyme. If pronunciation or vocabulary makes the relationship uncertain, skip the item as unsuitable rather than diagnosing a language difficulty.

The pairs below are proposed for ordinary English pronunciations; their audio and suitability for the pilot's children must be checked. A language or dialect change requires a reviewed bank, not word-for-word translation.

## Authored sequence

Pictures have no written words. Candidate order and correct position vary across independent trials.

| ID / role | Target and candidates in spoken order | Example prompt | Target answer | Evidence and next action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| R0 / invitation | Empty broadcast board | “Let's make a silly sound show. You can ask to hear the words again.” | None | Invitation only |
| R1 / entry opportunity | cat; hat, sun | “Listen: cat. Which rhymes with cat: hat or sun?” | hat | Initial independent recognition if the task is understood |
| RD / conditional demonstration | bee; tree, fish | “Bee and tree have the same ending sound. They rhyme. Bee and fish don't.” | None | Tutor models contrast; never score repetition as independent |
| R2 / fresh opportunity | dog; cup, log | “Which rhymes with dog: cup or log?” | log | Recognition; place the dog/log pair after accepted success |
| R3 / fresh opportunity | boat; coat, fish | “Which rhymes with boat: coat or fish?” | coat | New pair, correct position changed; add to board after success |
| R4 / optional fresh opportunity | fox; bed, box | “Which rhymes with fox: bed or box?” | box | Optional recognition opportunity; no production required |
| RE / ending | Completed sound-pair board | “The sound show is ready. Shall we finish with a bell or a drum?” | Either | Preference, short approved sound, clear ending |

**Branching:** use RD when the child does not understand “rhymes,” makes an interpretable mismatch, or requests help. Otherwise keep it available. End after R3 by default. Offer R4 only if the child wants another and two fresh opportunities have produced independent responses. Supported completion is allowed without passing all trials.

Two-option tasks permit guessing. A few correct choices do not prove reliable recognition. Do not change to open-ended rhyme production as an automatic difficulty increase; it has a different evidence contract.

## Response contract

- Accept the correct candidate alone or in a phrase, such as “the log.”
- Accept “first” or “second” only if the options were presented in a stable recorded order and the reference is unambiguous. Do not require ordinal vocabulary.
- A clearly understood developmental pronunciation can resolve to a candidate; ambiguous audio stays unverified. Avoid grading articulation.
- “Yes,” repetition of the target word, or naming both candidates is not a discriminating choice. Replay both options neutrally and ask for one.
- If the child offers a different valid rhyme, acknowledge the contribution but record it separately; for the selection task, invite a choice from the two candidates. Do not let the model invent an authoritative answer-key extension.
- If a caregiver supplies the answer, record supported participation rather than independent recognition.

## Support and control of error

1. Give thinking time; allow one neutral replay of the complete target/options prompt.
2. For an interpretable mismatch or help request, contrast both pairings at the same pace: “dog–cup; dog–log.” This is a scaffold and must be recorded.
3. If needed, model the shared ending sound and correct pairing, then let the show continue. Do not force an echo to unlock progress.

Allow at most two response opportunities after the initial attempt. Move on or end earlier when requested. If the child is exploring sounds rather than choosing, respond warmly and keep the observation unscored.

There is **no fully self-correcting visual material** for this target. Matching puzzle outlines would cue the correct choice without demonstrating rhyme recognition. Hearing the contrasted pairs can help the child inspect and repair, but correctness still depends on reviewed linguistic content and reliable response interpretation.

Preserve two options and comparable familiar vocabulary; unfamiliar or multisyllabic replacements require a new condition.

## Evidence, completion, and recap

Store the exact target/options, spoken order, pronunciation/audio version where available, picture labels, initial response, support actually heard, self-correction, and parent help. A vocabulary explanation before a trial affects interpretation and should remain visible.

Normal playback of the task words is part of the independent recognition task; contrastive coaching after an attempt is support. Recognition with spoken options is not independent recall or rhyme production.

**Example recap:** “Practiced hearing words that rhyme. Chose log for dog without a hint; used a sound contrast for boat–coat. A later fresh example would help us understand how consistent this is.” Use only when supported by recorded observations.

The [review policy](../11-skill-map-and-progression.md) permits other experiences while recognition remains uncertain. Never require rhyme success to access counting or shapes.

## Presentation and generation bounds

Use original clear pictures, minimal background noise, and no music under prompts. Optional sound effects occur after response settlement. The correct option must not be larger, brighter, animated, or placed in a predictably correct location.

The word bank, pronunciations, candidates, and answer keys are authored. AI may vary a short welcome or neutral acknowledgment, but must not improvise instructional rhymes, introduce a third option, or alter the evidence rules. If a word pair cannot be validated, use a reviewed fallback or omit it. Theme variants can change the radio framing while holding the linguistic task stable.

## Success criteria and findings

Continue if children understand the sound-selection task, candidate vocabulary is accessible, and the system reliably distinguishes their choices. Look for reduced support on fresh pairs without describing the small pilot as proof of reading improvement.

Revise if picture naming, accent handling, automatic repetition of the last word, position preference, or tutor prosody explains apparent success or failure. Check those confounds before adding more difficulty.

Use the [pilot plan](../12-pilot-and-validation.md) and #19 measurement. **Findings: not yet tested.** Audio review, live speech checks, educator review, and child playtesting remain required before any validation claim.
