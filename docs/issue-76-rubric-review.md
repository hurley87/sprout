## Shared attribution and closure rules

- **independent:** Use the student's own relevant turn(s). Neutral requests to explain, clarify or repeat the printed source are not answer-giving. Later praise cannot retroactively supply or prompt earlier evidence.
- **assisted:** A substantive tutor cue/explanation precedes the selected learner evidence and contributes to it. Require the learner's own explanation/application beyond a simple echo; attribute only affected criteria, not every criterion in the question.
- **partial:** Relevant but incomplete evidence. Keep criterion-level coverage; never fill missing propositions from tutor words or exemplar similarity.
- **incorrect:** An explicit learner claim conflicts with the criterion. Missing evidence, unavailable classification and ambiguous ASR are not incorrect. Current observer concept labels do not independently encode incorrect; implementing outcome projection must retain validated objective/attempt evidence.
- **unresolved:** Preserve uncertainty about meaning, attribution, ASR, source interpretation or unavailable assessment. Do not award mastery or manufacture an error. Reasoned Q5 historical uncertainty can coexist with complete application-of-test evidence.
- **skipped:** An explicit navigation action leaves unresolved/unattempted criteria or subparts behind. Preserve any prior evidence and the skip event separately; a skip does not erase demonstrated or partial work.
- **unattempted:** No substantive learner attempt exists for the question/subpart; distinguish never reached from reached then explicitly skipped using authored visit/navigation history.
- **selfCorrection:** Retain ordered learner turns and source revisions. Correction before substantive tutor answer-giving may remain independent. After a relevant hint, record assisted correction. Q10 uses the latest complete non-withdrawn proposal, not cumulative best evidence; other concepts keep cumulative evidence.
- **provenance:** Examples use one-based turn positions; runtime evidence must additionally keep actual runtime/node/visit/turn identity, transcript revision and exact selected learner text/span. Tutor turns are context only. Never use praise, source quotations, answer-key text or assent as student-authored explanation.
- **closure:** Coverage and closure are separate. A useful probe/help exchange may end with partial or incorrect evidence. Explicit next/skip follows authored navigation; I don't know alone is not navigation. Stop immediately releases media. No numeric marks or pass/fail.

### q8: Q8, complete question

**Spoken prompt:** What does a phonetic writing system represent, and what main advantage does it offer over a pictographic one?

**Reference answer (authored):**

Phonetic symbols represent speech sounds, whereas pictographic symbols picture things or concepts. A novice reader can combine the represented sounds to decode a written word they have not seen before but may already know in speech, instead of having to recognize a separate whole picture or sign. The manuscript links this to learning to read more quickly; it does not say mature phonetic readers are always faster.

- **sound-decoding** [required; source-backed; passages: phonetic]: Explains the central advantage: combine represented sounds to decode an unfamiliar written word, potentially recognizing an already-known spoken word. Saying easier, fewer symbols, or faster without this mechanism is partial, not full explanation.

**Review boundary:** Central advantage is explicit on printed p. 111, with learning-speed discussion on p. 112; no arbitrary instructor-only review gate.
