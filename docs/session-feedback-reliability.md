The next saved “Three” advances (0.99). The discrepancy suggests recognition error; neither transcript establishes what was spoken. ## Recovery policy

STAY is an application decision, not evidence that a child struggled. There is no provider recognition-confidence signal at the current boundary. Recovery uses a bounded, target-independent text policy:

- An isolated out-of-scope total (above five), a question, or unclear/non-isolated text gets neutral clarification: “I want to make sure I heard you. Could you say your number again?”
- A clear in-scope total or ascending count sequence can receive the existing counting hint.
- After the app releases clarification, repeating the same isolated total on the same scene can receive instructional help. A different out-of-scope total gets clarification again. Advancement clears the confirmation context.

This policy neither changes the transcript nor changes the evaluator threshold. Repetition is corroborating text, not proof of accurate recognition. `needs_confirmation` responses cannot support determinate Observer claims, even with mapped timing. A clarification request does not establish instructional support. No generated tutor text is promoted to delivered speech or support evidence.

## Clock and attribution contracts

Application `atMs` and observed transcript-arrival times use the application's receipt of `session.started` as zero. Provider speech offsets have their own clock and may be source-local after replacement. New utterances retain the legacy offsets and explicitly label them as `providerTiming`, including source identity when available.

`responseScene` captures the displayed scene when the first transcript fragment arrived. It remains attached when an answer advances the scene before durable utterance flush. Fragments received across different scenes mark that context as changed; provider-source changes split utterances. This establishes application response context, not the exact scene during acoustic speech.

`sessionTiming` now has three explicit provenances. Historical `mapped_provider` intervals and `source_input_bound` envelopes remain supported. The latter is a conservative source-input envelope, without a provider clock offset: each peer attaches **disabled cloned microphone tracks**, and LessonSession records a fence against the confirmed display before the transport enables those tracks. Pending replacement peers supply silence throughout warmup. The original microphone stays available for recording and VAD. Promotion retires the old clone and opens a new, independently identified fence only after source authority changes.

Disabled tracks supply silence to consumers under the [Media Capture specification](https://www.w3.org/TR/mediacapture-streams/#life-cycle). The bound contains the possible microphone input supplied to this source from its fence through the receipt of the last transcript fragment. It is deliberately not an exact acoustic utterance interval and does not resolve device capture latency, ASR accuracy or speaker identity. Arrival supplies only the causal upper bound. Neither provider offsets nor nearest local VAD bursts determine its start. Canonical evidence, display fences and bounds use the same monotonic browser performance clock, rounded outward for bounds; the application's Live-ready event is zero. Recording now retains its actual start offset in that clock rather than assuming synchronous capture starts exactly at zero.

Production BrowserTransport also attaches an application-owned `sourceRequestedAt` to transcript events: the monotonic browser timestamp immediately before the `/api/live` request that creates that specific source. Provider JSON cannot supply or overwrite it. Source creation cannot precede that request. The [GPT-Live session guide](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/live-conversations) documents transcript offsets as approximate fragment positions measured in milliseconds from the beginning of the provider session. It explicitly warns that arrival is uneven and fragments do not establish completed turns. Context acknowledgments are estimated context-delivery positions, not speech/playback or microphone-queue fences; this implementation does not use them for attribution.

`source_timeline_bound` combines these facts without assigning an exact provider/browser offset:

- `sourceRequestedAtMs` is the browser creation-request timestamp relative to canonical zero, and can be negative on the initial source. `inputOpenedAtMs` and `inputScene` retain the independently established microphone fence and initial display identity.
- The earliest approximate session-timeline start is `max(inputOpenedAtMs, floor(sourceRequestedAtMs + providerTiming.startMs))`. A provider session cannot already have elapsed that much time before its creation request. Startup/network delay makes this lower bound earlier and more conservative; the app does not subtract an assumed RTT or assert that the source started at request time.
- The latest bound remains the receipt of the final transcript fragment, rounded outward. We do **not** map `providerTiming.endMs` to an exact acoustic end or assume the provider timeline never stalls. Delayed delivery widens the envelope.
- Each fragment must have a valid bound from the same source, creation request and input fence. The accumulator retains the earliest provider offset and bound across corrections and the latest receipt. Missing/invalid bounds cannot regain trust from later fragments. Impossible future starts, malformed offsets and source mismatches supply no trusted interval.

A concrete claim requires one uniquely displayed scene over the **entire** envelope, stable arrival context, finalized speech and clear recognition. When the lower bound is still the original microphone fence, its explicit display identity disambiguates the display/input-enable timestamp tie; another display at that timestamp remains ambiguous. When provider elapsed time narrows the lower bound beyond that fence, subsequent scenes can establish concrete evidence on the same source. No source/input fence resets at display changes. A transition path that ignores answer-window fragments now retains its canonical source fence rather than deleting it. Advancing after receipt does not invalidate an already bounded response to the prior scene.

Event support must strictly precede the earliest possible response start; recording support must **end** strictly before that start for either conservative bound provenance. Help arriving inside the envelope cannot be called prior assistance. Missing source identity, mismatched sources or a fragment without a valid bound cannot inherit trust from another fragment.

These are bounds around **documented approximate provider fragment timing**, not a calibrated acoustic/word alignment or proof of ASR accuracy, audio-device latency, speaker identity or transcript completeness. A large creation/setup delay, a stalled provider timeline, or a delayed transcript whose envelope crosses a display can still prevent a concrete claim. There is no guarantee that every response will be concrete. The fixed first-scene-only restriction is removed: ordinary successive responses with bounds wholly inside successive scenes can now support observations without additional paid replacements. The historical butterfly `50600` provider / `43075` arrival / `43640` display case remains uncertain without independently recorded anchors; no saved records are backfilled.

### Parent correction

Use `sessionSpeechInterval(evidence)` to validate/retrieve either historical mapped timing or a conservative production envelope. The new provenance includes its source request and input-fence anchors, and validation verifies the derived lower bound against the retained provider offset, source identity and receipt envelope. `responseSceneValidity(responseEvidence, citedSceneEvent, canonicalEvents)` is the reusable scene check now used by canonical Observer validation. It filters display events itself and returns `valid` plus individual interval/transition/scene/arrival-attribution checks. Call it with the full canonical event set, not just the cited scene: otherwise intervening or competing displays are invisible. Parent correction now uses this same helper with the full canonical event set. Concrete corrections require its `valid` result; uncertain corrections retain missing/conflicting scene reasons, and conflicting arrival attribution specifically retains `conflicting_context`. The canonical target remains tied to the cited display. Explicit parent testimony can still correct speech interpretation, speaker identity and recognition uncertainty or report assistance in `parent_review` context; it cannot create scene facts or recorded support. Observer finalization, speaker, recognition and support rules remain separate from scene validity. The helper does not repair legacy records.

The Convex evidence union accepts the optional new timing shape. `sessions.appendEvent` reuses `sessionSpeechInterval` to reject mismatched source/anchor/derived-start/receipt bounds before storing them. The legacy reject-only resolution, planning exclusion, immutable decisions, corruption validation and stale-snapshot checks remain in place.

Existing saved sessions and proposals are never rewritten. Legacy timing remains inspectable under the separately implemented parent-review resolution policy. New concrete claims must meet the strict current timing contract.

Playback seeks the earliest session bound when available (which may precede the spoken answer for either conservative envelope). Otherwise it uses first transcript arrival as an approximate review anchor, then the canonical event time. It never seeks using an unlabelled/provider speech offset.

## Verification and boundaries

Observer attempts now retain an immutable input snapshot and bounded coverage/validation diagnostics in separate attempt and child-row tables. Coverage compares every saved non-Sprout utterance in that input with the entire returned proposal batch, including rejected proposals. A declared canonical event reference to an actual saved response counts as returned even when its role or claim is rejected; malformed/unmappable proposals retain their ordinal, any bounded proposal identity, and validation paths/reasons without invented event links. Response rows retain event, fragment and evaluation-event joins where available, speaker/finalization/timing status, and full reference counts. Trace details are capped at eight items and text at 200 UTF-8 bytes, with explicit truncation flags; counts and coverage are computed before truncation. The existing 1,000-event/proposal limits and five-attempt limit bound persistence. Detailed rows are paginated behind the existing parent-review server capability; the review inspection read includes attempt summaries, not raw provider payloads, tokens or snapshots.

An explicit empty usable proposal batch records absent responses. Provider, transport, refused/incomplete/malformed-envelope and recording failures before usable output record unknown coverage and the failure stage (recording, provider setup, transcription or proposal) instead; an invalid or oversized proposal batch records a batch rejection and unknown coverage. Known incomplete records, absent recordings and missing trustworthy response timing are separate facts, never inferred causes of omission. Absent-output causes remain `unknown`. Provider validation collects the bounded whole-batch report before throwing. Successful publication saves independently computed backend diagnostics atomically with the complete proposal batch. If publication throws, its transaction still rolls back; the action's owned, unexpired `fail` mutation revalidates the attempted batch against the original snapshot and persists diagnostics with failure. Changed inputs retain original-snapshot coverage and an explicit changed-snapshot flag. Settled reports are write-once, retries keep prior attempt/snapshot provenance, and stale/expired workers cannot attach reports to a newer attempt. An expired worker with no captured result remains `not_captured`; this does not assert that the provider returned nothing. Ready analyses remain immutable. Diagnostic rows never enter parent decisions, reviewed evidence or planning, and do not change success/failure or review-completeness policy.

Local fixtures establish several possible omission paths: the provider may return no proposal for a saved utterance; canonical response/scene/timing material may be insufficient; or proposal validation may reject the whole batch before publication. The saved-record pipeline tests distinguish these outcomes without a billed provider call. They do not establish why historical butterfly/strawberry observations were missing. Determining that cause still requires the original attempt's exact input snapshot, complete returned proposal batch (including any rejected proposals), provider/transport completion state, and validation diagnostics; acoustic/scene timing claims additionally require independently trustworthy timing anchors. Historical analyses with no diagnostic attempts are explicitly `legacy_unavailable`, with missing attempt numbers reported; no original records, proposals or decisions are rewritten or reconstructed.
