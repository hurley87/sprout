# Session feedback semantics

Catching Unicorns uses `SessionSummary` for both recap rendering and tutor context. It is a deterministic projection of session-local accepted live evidence and the asynchronous full-conversation review, not a new model-generated assessment. The summary includes up to two recorded strengths, one practice focus, and an authored exercise adapted to that focus. There is no numerical grade.

## Evidence policy

- Authored criterion IDs identify concepts. Repeated scene criteria collapse into one concept, retaining every scene record, quotation, prompting history, full-review outcome and score in provenance/export. Repetition never creates extra strengths.
- A strength requires an accepted live demonstration with its attributable learner quotation, or confidently demonstrated full-review content with validated learner references from this attempt and assessment snapshot. It describes a recorded explanation, not global mastery. Review labels without valid provenance cannot supply a strength.
- Uncertain understanding review results abstain: they do not erase a recorded explanation or diagnose failure. Strength text explicitly qualifies an inconclusive full review. Confident partial/not-yet review outcomes conflicting with a demonstration keep the concept open and exclude it from strengths. Conflicting confident results across repeated scene criteria also remain open.
- Prompting is conservative: prompted evidence from either source takes precedence over independent attribution. Unknown live prompting stays unclear. Assistance uncertainty alone does not make demonstrated content inconclusive; independent wording names its live or review source. Unknown prompting on an accepted live demonstration stays unclear; review-only independence requires confident independent assistance on every supporting demonstration. Neither uncertain review results nor missing observations prove a knowledge deficit.
- Practice selection uses the explicit instructional tiers below. Missing observations are exploration opportunities, not diagnosed deficits. Exercises invite an observable explanation and comparison against the appropriate source, without disclosing an unresolved canonical answer.
- CAF concepts and exercises are explicitly transfer reasoning. They never become assertions that the manuscript establishes facts about the CAF.

## Deterministic instructional selection

`lib/lesson-runtime/catching-unicorns-feedback.ts` owns one entry per collapsed concept ID: a strength tier, an ordinal instructional priority, an optional foundational flag, prerequisite concept IDs, and a concrete exercise. These are authored pedagogical choices, not additional assessment fields. The strength tier describes the existing rubric's demand: reasoning relationships/causes, applying a framework, explaining a property, or identifying/recognizing a definition or example. A recorded demonstration is the eligibility gate. Metadata alone never establishes understanding or proves the quality of a particular reply; neither transcript length, keywords, titles nor repeated observations affect rank.

Strengths select at most two **recorded** concepts, sorting reasoning before application before explanation before definition/recognition. Within a tier, lower authored priority comes first; equal priorities use ascending concept ID (plain string comparison, independent of locale). Memory-extension reasoning has the first reasoning priority, ahead of synthesis; a basic definition cannot displace it just because it occurred earlier. Disagreement and unsupported review-only labels remain ineligible. Quotations and attribution still come from the unchanged reconciliation projection; ranking never rewrites provenance.

Practice uses these tiers, in order:

1. Supported partial/disputed foundational understanding (engram, exogram, meaningful visual symbols).
2. Other partial/disputed understanding. Authored priority selects among these substantive gaps before uncertainty about prompting.
3. An unobserved non-foundational topic with explicit, nonempty prerequisites, **all recorded without conflict**. This is an extension opportunity, not a deficit. Discovery requires recorded engram, exogram and memory-extension understanding. Thus a clarified exogram plus supported intermediate-step reasoning can lead to discovery practice even when assistance is unclear. An unresolved foundational gap still takes precedence.
4. Recorded understanding with prompted or unclear assistance: practice explaining without a cue, without calling the understanding deficient.
5. Other unobserved topics: explore the earliest authored priority, usually an unresolved foundational topic in an empty session. A failed prerequisite gate does not suppress a supported gap or diagnose the prerequisite; it only prevents advanced extension priority. Prerequisites are direct readiness checks, not transitive mastery gates: a supported memory-extension explanation remains usable even if an earlier visual-symbol definition was not observed.
6. When everything is independently recorded, extend a concept whose prerequisites are recorded, using the strength tiers and authored priorities (normally memory extension). Recorded topics whose prerequisites are not recorded are the final fallback.

Each practice tier uses the same lower-priority-then-concept-ID tie-break. Evidence object/record insertion order has no instructional meaning. Pending/unavailable/idle review uses accepted live evidence with the same rules and existing notices. Uncertain content abstains; assistance uncertainty does not turn recorded understanding into partial understanding. Unsupported review provenance cannot unlock a prerequisite or strength. Confident conflicts remain visible and receive a task comparing the differing explanations.

Every concept has its own learner action and observable explanation. Definition tasks ask the learner to classify a concrete example and explain its location or meaning. Comparison tasks use a note, instruction or revision. Reasoning/discovery tasks ask for a small written/drawn example, a relationship or intermediate step, and an explanation of how the representation helps. Culture tasks compare an example with a specific framework characteristic; CAF tasks require checkable evidence and its limits, explicitly marked as transfer. Tasks ask questions rather than supplying unresolved canonical answers.

A small evidence-category prefix adapts the task: partial means clarify/change the earlier account; disagreement means compare and test the differing accounts; unobserved means explore without assuming a gap; prompted/unclear means explain without a cue; independent means extend with a fresh example. Source tasks then ask for checking against the Preface or Introduction; transfer tasks do not assert CAF facts. Both screen and tutor receive this same exercise. Full quotes stay in the evidence disclosure, keeping the existing 2,000-byte context budget.

## Full-review contract v2 and migration

`conversation-assessment-v2` separates two full-review dimensions per authored scene criterion. The provider receives two existing structured `type: "choice"` questions, keyed by `nodeId:criterionId:understanding` and `nodeId:criterionId:assistance`. The understanding and assistance schema and thresholds are unchanged. New requests additionally use the `learner-references-v1` evidence contract described below. Older v2 responses remain diagnostic/live-feedback compatible.

- Understanding chooses `demonstrated`, `partial`, or `not_yet` (absent relevant learner explanation). Normalization returns `uncertain` when that dimension cannot meet the existing confidence gate.
- Assistance chooses `independent`, `prompted`, or `unclear`. Normalization also returns `unclear` on abstention. `abstained` distinguishes a confident provider choice of unclear from an inconclusive assistance distribution.
- Each dimension retains its own raw `choice`, provider `confidence`, and unchanged probabilities in `scores`, together with its normalized `outcome` and `abstained` flag. As in the existing observer, the gate uses selected probability >= 0.9, each competitor <= 0.2, and margin >= 0.7; provider confidence is retained diagnostically. The thresholds are unchanged and not newly calibrated.

Instructions judge cumulative learner explanation separately from its assistance history. A neutral request to elaborate, clarify meaning or give an example does not supply answer content and alone cannot imply prompting. Relevant answer-giving/scaffolding supplies criterion content, a leading answer, or a reasoning step before the learner explains it. Confirmation after the learner supplies an idea is not answer-giving. Tutor-only content, assent and simple echoes do not establish learner understanding or independent attribution. Accurate learner explanations after scaffolding may establish demonstrated content with prompted assistance.

The assessment API expects the dimension and reference question keys and returns v2 with an explicit evidence contract. The runtime revalidates scores and recomputes outcomes at a single compatibility boundary before publishing/exporting; claimed outcomes and ungrounded extra evidence fields are never promoted. Missing/invalid v2 provider dimensions, unknown versions, wrong/extra criterion keys, invalid distributions, mixed source contracts and provider/model failures make review unavailable while preserving the conversation and live feedback. Pending, idle and unavailable runtime states also carry the contract version.

Unversioned/v1 saved answers or old normalized API responses can migrate to v2 with `sourceContract: "legacy-v1"` and untouched `legacyScores`. Understanding aggregates `demonstrated_independent + demonstrated_prompted`, keeping partial and not-yet mass separate, and applies the same gate. Both derived dimensions retain the original four-way confidence diagnostically; it is not a separately assessed marginal confidence. The original distribution and confidence also remain in `legacyScores`. Assistance maps the two demonstration masses to independent/prompted and the remaining partial/not-yet mass to unclear, retaining the original confidence and applying the same gate without conditional rescaling. Thus 0.52 independent + 0.47 prompted + 0.01 partial supports demonstrated content but unclear assistance. Ambiguous content still abstains, and partial/absent evidence cannot prove independence. A migrated response is revalidated from its raw legacy scores, avoiding repeated aggregation or invented claims.

The recap's evidence disclosure shows understanding and assistance separately. Summary disagreement and inconclusive-content handling use understanding only; confidently prompted assistance conservatively qualifies live demonstrations. This migration does not change strength/practice ranking, live classification, accepted live records, progression/reveal gates, or scene navigation.

## Multi-message provenance and reconciliation

New API requests include an application-owned runtime ID and positive assessment generation. The review snapshot saves that ownership and the complete submitted ordered visits. A reference `visit_<visitId>_message_<index>` addresses the parsed learner utterance in that snapshot, with consecutive same-speaker fragments merged using the existing transcript parser. The provider receives the full conversation and a single application-generated `learnerReferences` table of IDs, node/visit/message identities and exact parsed text. Each reference is resolved again from the saved transcript for feedback; provider-generated quotation fields are ignored. The full original transcript remains available as context, and multiple replies render as separate quotations, never fabricated concatenated speech.

Each criterion has four additional existing structured Choice questions (`:evidence_0` through `:evidence_3`). Choices are the application-provided reference IDs, `none`, and `overflow`. References must form a unique prefix in submitted conversation order, followed by `none`. Instructions require `overflow` in the final slot when more than four replies are needed; any selected overflow makes review unavailable rather than silently truncating it. All four distributions retain raw scores and must meet the unchanged confidence gate for grounding. A valid all-none selection is missing provenance; a distribution that abstains has uncertain provenance. Neither supports a review-only strength. Requests above 64 eligible learner utterances return 422 without sending a provider request; serialized provider payloads above 1.5 MB also fail explicitly. Conversation limits remain 100 visits and 120,000 transcript characters. These are conservative application bounds, not verified provider limits.

Reference candidates exclude tutor messages, basic assent/filler, simple movement requests and literal echoes of the preceding tutor message. Each reference is validated against the complete owning snapshot, visit and parsed message identity, role and eligibility. Runtime parsing and the feedback projection require exact runtime ID, assessment generation and ordered visit/transcript equality with the independently supplied request snapshot captured locally; foreign or stale responses cannot ground evidence. Claimed grounding and understanding/assistance outcomes are recomputed from closed distributions. Invalid IDs, duplicates, wrong order, gaps, missing questions or malformed distributions make review unavailable. Unknown evidence contracts fail closed. Legacy v1 and earlier v2 responses without references retain their scores and diagnostic judgments, but never acquire quotation support or create a review-only strength.

`SessionSummary` is a separate reconciled feedback projection. It retains every accepted live record unchanged, alongside review dimensions, reference scores, resolved quotations and a per-record reconciliation disposition. Deterministic precedence is:

1. Accepted live demonstration remains usable when review is pending/unavailable, content is uncertain, or review provenance is missing. Review assistance uncertainty alone does not negate supported content.
2. Confident review demonstration with grounded references can supply a review-only explanation where no attributable live observation exists. It can clarify a **tentative** live partial only if at least one supporting reply occurs later than the live source in the exact saved conversation; the earlier source must match its saved node, visit, message index and text. Both observations remain disclosed as `later-clarification`.
3. A confident supported live partial/not-yet is kept as a conflict with positive review, even with later replies. A tentative partial without a verified later supporting reply also remains open. The live contract does not distinguish incomplete from inaccurate confident partials, so this policy conservatively preserves both. Missing/mismatched earlier provenance cannot be declared superseded.
4. Confident negative review judgments conflicting with a live or grounded review demonstration, including repeated criteria in different scenes, exclude a strength and stay visible as disagreement. Legacy negative judgments retain their conservative veto. A later positive label never silently removes a supported contradiction.
5. Content uncertainty, assistance uncertainty and provenance uncertainty are separate diagnostics. Prompted evidence conservatively takes precedence; no supported independent assistance means prompting stays unclear. Raw live prompting history, original scores and review source attribution remain in export.

Review does not mutate accepted live evidence, progressive reveals, navigation, classifier thresholds or mastery gates. Strength/practice selection uses the lesson-owned metadata above with the existing two-strength limit. Later slices own broader recap copy/presentation, tutor probes and integrated provider-backed evaluation.

These guards establish provenance and deterministic reconciliation, not semantic correctness. Reference selection and completeness are also model judgments; an overflow instruction cannot deterministically prove all necessary replies were selected. A substantive learner reply may still be irrelevant, an inexact echo, incomplete or contradictory; the provider must judge it against the rubric. The exogram follow-up can be used when the rubric judgment supports it. Merely locating the specialists/universities answer does not prove the idea-discoverers criterion. Mocks verify contract behavior rather than live model quality, and no paid provider calls validate this slice. Four slots and the candidate/request bounds may leave long sessions without full review; their accepted live feedback and complete saved conversation remain useful.

## Delivery and lifecycle

The runtime creates the summary when recap rendering is confirmed. It starts full review without waiting for it, and appends the actual feedback instead of generic screen/question steering. The tutor is instructed to give the strengths, practice focus and exercise, and use that same content for subsequent feedback/study-next requests. It must not claim to see unsupplied screen content. The screen receives the same model through the runtime snapshot; standalone presentation tests may derive the same projection.

Review completion or failure refreshes the model. A passive tutor-context update waits behind any pending scene steering acknowledgment. It explicitly forbids an extra turn or speech interruption, preserves the entry recap if still due, and is deduplicated. This does not introduce a new scene/audio gate or change conversation transition coordination. Concise feedback text is supplied within the existing context append budget; full quotations and raw scores remain in evidence details/export rather than being repeated into tutor instructions.

Pending and unavailable review states keep useful live-evidence feedback. Each runtime owns its evidence, assessment generation, summary and delivery key. A changed conversation supersedes/aborts the older assessment; generation checks reject late results even if abort is ignored. Stop may finish a new full-conversation review for the export and screen, but never appends feedback to a closed transport. The existing UI callback identity guard prevents an old runtime from publishing into a restarted session.

## Recap presentation

The recap uses one readable column for the shared strengths, practice focus, and next exercise. Quotations stay inside the native keyboard-accessible evidence disclosure. Concepts appear once; identical quotations within a concept appear once, with every scene observation, independent/prompted/unclear attribution, review outcome, and learner-turn source retained underneath. Different quotations remain separate. CAF entries keep their transfer label. Raw scores and prompting history remain available in the session export.

“Finish discussion” calls the existing session stop lifecycle. It releases the live transport and capture while retaining the recap and its evidence disclosure; “Start new discussion” begins a separate attempt. Export remains under Session details after finishing. Empty, pending, and unavailable feedback keep the same evidence policy and do not imply mastery or failure. CAF and synthesis questions name the framework/topics without supplying unresolved answers; tutor closure acknowledges and pauses for app-owned advancement without a readiness question. Learner follow-ups remain supported.
